Cloth simulation.

A grid of point masses joined by springs, stepped with Verlet integration in 3D and shaded on the GPU. The springs stay drawn on top, so you can watch the math hold the fabric together. Grab it, fling it, slice it, or orbit around it.

0 points 0 springs 0 fps 0 ms/frame

This demo needs WebGL, which your browser has turned off.

Drag a point to pull and throw Drag empty space to orbit Right-drag or Shift+drag to cut Double-click a point to pin or unpin it Keys: G grab · C cut · W wind · T tearing · S self-collision · R reset
01 · Integrate

Verlet integration

Each point stores only its current and previous position. Velocity is implied by the difference, so a step is x' = x + (x − x₀)·damping + g·dt². No velocities to drift out of sync, and constraints can move points directly.

02 · Constrain

Three kinds of spring

Structural springs join neighbours and stop stretching. Shear springs cross each cell diagonally so squares can't skew into diamonds. Bend springs skip a point, resisting sharp folds; they're what makes the cloth feel sturdy. Turn on Shear & bend to see them. For self-collision, a spatial hash finds points that are close in space but far apart on the cloth and pushes them apart, and no point may move more than a set distance per step, so fast motion can't tunnel through.

03 · Shade

Lighting the surface

Every frame, each point's normal is the cross product of the vectors to its neighbours. A WebGL shader lights the fabric from that normal, with a soft wrap-around key light, a fill light and a sheen at grazing angles. The front is white and the back slate so you can see it turn over, and both blend toward orange wherever the springs are stretched, on the same scale as the lines.

Original

I first wrote this in Java four years ago as a desktop GUI, with a custom rendering engine built on Swing's 2D graphics drawing the cloth every frame. The original is on Replit ↗. This port keeps the cloth in flat typed arrays, uses a square-root-free spring correction, interpolates between physics steps for high-refresh displays, puts the solver to sleep once the cloth settles, and hands all the shading to the GPU.
